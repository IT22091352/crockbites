instructions:
  - title: "Step 1 – Trim and season the pork"
    text: "Remove any white silver skin membrane from the surface of the pork tenderloins with a sharp knife. Pat dry and rub with the salt, black pepper, rosemary, and thyme."
  - title: "Step 2 – Quick sear (optional for extra flavor)"
    text: "Heat olive oil in a skillet over high heat. Sear each tenderloin for 1 to 2 minutes per side until lightly browned on the outside. Transfer into the slow cooker."
  - title: "Step 3 – Mix the glaze"
    text: "In a bowl, whisk together the soy sauce, balsamic vinegar, honey, and minced garlic. Pour the glaze directly over the pork in the slow cooker."
  - title: "Step 4 – Slow cook on Low"
    text: "Cover and cook strictly on Low for 2.5 to 3 hours. Insert a meat thermometer into the thickest center: pull the pork the moment it registers 145°F (63°C). A light blush of pink in the center is safe and ensures maximum juiciness."
  - title: "Step 5 – Glaze and slice"
    text: "Transfer the tenderloins to a cutting board, tent with foil, and rest for 10 minutes. Pour the pan juices into a small saucepan, whisk in the cornstarch slurry, and simmer for 2 minutes until glossy and thick. Slice pork into 0.5-inch medallions and spoon the warm glaze over top."

servingSuggestions:

tips:
  - "Pork tenderloin (fillet) is the most tender cut on the pig, but it has almost no fat. Cooking it beyond 3 hours or on High will make it tough. Always use a thermometer and aim for 145°F."
  - "Resting the meat for 10 minutes before slicing is crucial to allow the internal juices to redistribute so they don't pool onto the cutting board."

### Why This Recipe Works
Pork tenderloin is an extremely lean muscle that requires precise timing rather than all-day stewing. Cooking it on Low for just under 3 hours in a sweet-and-tangy honey balsamic braise allows the meat to reach a juicy 145°F while absorbing rich umami depth without becoming dry or chalky.
