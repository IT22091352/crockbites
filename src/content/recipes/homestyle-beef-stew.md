instructions:
  - title: "Step 1 – Sear the beef cubes"
    text: "Toss the beef cubes with the flour, 1 tsp salt, and 0.5 tsp pepper. Heat olive oil in a heavy skillet over medium-high heat. Sear beef in batches for 2 to 3 minutes per side until a deep brown crust develops. Transfer seared beef into the slow cooker."
  - title: "Step 2 – Deglaze the skillet"
    text: "Pour the red wine into the hot skillet, scraping up all browned fond from the bottom with a wooden spoon. Stir in the tomato paste and Worcestershire sauce, then pour the mixture over the beef."
  - title: "Step 3 – Add vegetables and broth"
    text: "Add the potatoes, carrots, celery, onion, and minced garlic into the slow cooker. Pour the beef broth over everything. Tuck the bay leaves, crushed rosemary, and thyme into the liquid."
  - title: "Step 4 – Slow cook until fork-tender"
    text: "Cover and cook on Low for 7 to 8 hours (or High for 4 to 5 hours). The beef should yield effortlessly to a fork, reaching around 200°F (93°C) internally where tough collagen fully dissolves into gelatin."
  - title: "Step 5 – Finish with peas"
    text: "Discard the bay leaves. Stir in the frozen green peas during the final 15 minutes of cooking. Taste and adjust salt and pepper before ladling into bowls."

### Why This Recipe Works
Chuck roast is loaded with connective collagen. Over 7 to 8 hours on low heat, that tough collagen breaks down into rich, natural gelatin that coats the meat fibers and thickens the broth naturally. Searing the floured beef first locks in roasty Maillard depth that a raw dump-and-cook method simply cannot match.
